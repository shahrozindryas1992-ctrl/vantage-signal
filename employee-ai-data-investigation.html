HUMAN RISK / AI WORKPLACE

When an employee puts company data into an AI tool.

The shadow-AI problem is often a human behavior problem first — and a governance problem second.

Employees use AI because it is useful. Security risk appears when the organization has never defined what “useful” is allowed to include.

The accidental data leak

An employee may paste a client email, internal document, source code, HR information or other sensitive material into an AI tool to summarize, rewrite or analyze it.

The employee may not think of that action as sharing company data. From a security perspective, however, the data has crossed a boundary.

EMPLOYEEhas a task
AI TOOLreceives data
DATAmay leave the normal workflow
GOVERNANCEmust define the boundary

Why policies alone are not enough

A policy that simply says “do not use AI” may be ignored when employees have legitimate reasons to use AI. Better governance explains which tools are approved, what information is restricted, how outputs must be reviewed and how concerns are escalated.

The human-risk layer

This is not an argument that employees are careless. It is an argument that organizations should design workflows around predictable human behavior.

The secure choice should be clear before the employee has to make it.

Our takeaway

AI governance works best when it connects policy, approved tools, data classification, training and practical review. The goal is not to stop useful AI. It is to make responsible use easier than accidental exposure.
