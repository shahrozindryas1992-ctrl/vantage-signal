HUMAN RISK / SOCIAL ENGINEERING

The human decisions attackers look for.

Attackers do not always need to defeat a security system. Sometimes they only need a person to make one reasonable-looking decision at the wrong moment.

Human risk is not the same thing as blaming employees. It is about understanding the decisions an attacker is trying to influence.

The attack path can begin with a normal action

Opening a document, approving a login, forwarding a message, sharing a password, clicking a familiar-looking link or accepting an urgent request can all look ordinary in isolation.

The problem appears when an attacker has designed the situation around that action.

What attackers look for

URGENCYMake the person act quickly.
AUTHORITYMake the request feel legitimate.
FAMILIARITYUse names, brands or routines.
FRICTIONExploit processes people cannot easily verify.

Why onboarding and offboarding matter

Human risk is also procedural. New employees need clear expectations and the right access. Departing employees need access removed, devices recovered and responsibilities transferred.

A weak process can leave behind credentials, accounts or knowledge that an attacker can exploit later.

The practical question

Instead of asking whether employees are “security aware,” organizations should ask: Which decisions would be easiest for an attacker to influence here?

That changes the conversation from blame to design. Better processes, clear escalation routes and sensible verification steps can make the secure decision the easy decision.

Our takeaway

The strongest human-risk program is not one that expects people never to make mistakes. It is one that assumes mistakes will happen and builds systems that limit the damage.
